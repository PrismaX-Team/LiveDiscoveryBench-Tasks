Protein stability: a three-round mutation campaign

Across three feedback rounds, select single-amino-acid mutations for ten anonymized proteins, learn from returned measurements, and recommend validated and prospective variants.

Objective score · higher is better

01

Research task

Run a budget-constrained protein-stability campaign. Start from the visible candidate space and pilot assays, query three experimental batches through validation, revise the strategy from measured outcomes, then submit validated and prospective recommendations.

02

Why it remains worth advancing

Protein sequence space is far too large to test exhaustively. Better low-resource search strategies can shorten the path from experimental evidence to useful stabilizing mutations.

The most effective strategy for balancing exploration, exploitation and calibration under a fixed experimental budget remains unknown, leaving measurable room for continued progress.

Task workflow

How one evaluation run works

  1. 01

    Receive the campaign inputs

    Ten anonymized proteins, 3,953 allowed single mutants, 399 pilot measurements, assay guidance and campaign constraints are visible from the start.

  2. 02

    Run three feedback rounds

    Each round selects 160 new variants—16 per protein. Validation returns measurements only for that batch, and variants cannot be queried twice.

  3. 03

    Deliver an auditable handoff

    Submit each round’s batch, plate map and hypothesis ledger, then 80 measured recommendations, 80 unmeasured recommendations and a final report.

  4. 04

    Freeze and score once

    After submission is frozen, test reads the trusted validation history and fixed hidden study to compute the deterministic 0–100 raw task score.

ScoreA deterministic 0–100 score combining closed-loop discovery, final portfolios, learning, calibration and operational validity; higher is better.
